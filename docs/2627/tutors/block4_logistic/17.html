RQ: How are current intentions to quit smoking associated with the probability that an individual has previously attempted to quit smoking?

Data dictionary:

variable description
S Subject Number
Q Whether the subject made an attempt to quit using tobacco products (0 = no attempt to quit; 1 = attempted to quit).
I Subject's intention to quit (1 = Never intend to quit; 2 = May intend to quit eventually; 3 = Intend to quit in the next 6 months; 4 = Intend to quit in the next 30 days)

Set up and fit model

Question 4

The logistic regression model we will fit has the formula QuitAttempt ~ Intent.

Based on the contrast matrix you generated above, write the mathematical expression that represents this model.

🗂️ See Logistic regression example > Mathematical model formulation flash card.

Solution

Solution 4. \[ \text{logodds(QuitAttempt)} = \beta_0 + (\beta_1 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Maybe}) + (\beta_2 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes6mo}) + (\beta_3 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes30days}) \]

Write:

$$
\text{logodds(QuitAttempt)} = \beta_0 + 
(\beta_1 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Maybe}) + 
(\beta_2 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes6mo}) + 
(\beta_3 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes30days})
$$

Question 5

For each \(\beta\) coefficient in the model expression above, write a sentence to describe what it will mean.

🗂️ See Logistic regression example > Interpreting the coefficients flash card.

Solution

Solution 5.

  • \(\beta_0\) (the intercept): the estimated log-odds of having previously attempted to quit smoking for people who currently intend to never quit smoking.
  • \(\beta_1\): the difference in the log-odds of having a previous quit attempt between people who intend to maybe quit smoking and people who intend to never quit smoking.
  • \(\beta_2\): the difference in the log-odds of having a previous quit attempt between people who intend to quit smoking within the next six months and people who intend to never quit smoking.
  • \(\beta_3\): the difference in the log-odds of having a previous quit attempt between people who intend to quit smoking within the next 30 days and people who intend to never quit smoking.

Question 6

Use glm() to fit this logistic regression model. Save the output as quit_mdl and generate the model summary.

🗂️ See Logistic regression example > Fit model flash card.

Question 7

For each coefficient estimated in quit_mdl, write a sentence that interprets it and another sentence that reports its statistical significance. (Remember that for logistic regression, we want to report the significance of the intercept as well because now it tests whether a particular outcome is significantly more or less likely than random chance!)

🗂️ See Logistic regression example > Interpreting the coefficients flash card.

Solution

Solution 7. (Intercept):

  • The log-odds of having a previous quit attempt for somebody who currently never intends to quit smoking is –1.95; this corresponds to a probability of about 12.5%.
  • This estimate (in log-odds space) is not significantly different from zero, that is, from 50% chance (\(p\) = .069).

IntentMaybe

  • The log-odds of having a previous quit attempt for somebody who currently intends to maybe quit smoking is estimated to increase by 2.58 log-odds compared to someone who never intends to quit.
  • This estimate is significantly different from zero (\(p\) = .024).

IntentYes6mo

  • The log-odds of having a previous quit attempt for somebody who currently intends to quit smoking within the next six months is estimated to increase by 3.23 log-odds compared to someone who never intends to quit.
  • This estimate is significantly different from zero (\(p\) = .006).

IntentYes30days

  • The log-odds of having a previous quit attempt for somebody who currently intends to quit smoking within the next 30 months is estimated to increase by 3.33 log-odds compared to someone who never intends to quit.
  • This estimate is significantly different from zero (\(p\) = .031).

Predict probabilities of quit attempts

Question 8

What is the predicted log-odds of each group (that is, each level of Intent) to have previously attempted to quit smoking?

Use the mathematical model expression from Q4 and the contrast matrix from Q2 to calculate the model-predicted log-odds by hand (that is, not using predict()).

🗂️ See Logistic regression example > Predicting probabilities for specific predictor values flash card.

Solution

Solution 8. The mathematical model expression, with the model-estimated coefficients substituted in (and “QuitAttempt” shortened to “QA” for convenience), is:

\[ \text{logodds(QA)} = –1.95 + (2.58 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Maybe}) + (3.23 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes6mo}) + (3.33 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes30days}) \]

The contrast matrix tells us what values to substitute in for each predictor in order to calculate the estimated log-odds of having a previous quit attempt for each level of Intent.

contrasts(quit_data$Intent)
          Maybe Yes6mo Yes30days
Never         0      0         0
Maybe         1      0         0
Yes6mo        0      1         0
Yes30days     0      0         1


For Intent = Never, the values we substitute in for each dummy variable are:

contrasts(quit_data$Intent)['Never',]
    Maybe    Yes6mo Yes30days 
        0         0         0

So:

\[ \begin{align} \text{logodds(QA)}_\text{Never} &= –1.95 + (2.58 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Maybe}) + (3.23 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes6mo}) + (3.33 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes30days}) \\ &= –1.95 + (2.58 \cdot 0 ) + (3.23 \cdot 0 ) + (3.33 \cdot 0 ) \\ &= –1.95 \\ \end{align} \]


For Intent = Maybe:

contrasts(quit_data$Intent)['Maybe',]
    Maybe    Yes6mo Yes30days 
        1         0         0

So:

\[ \begin{align} \text{logodds(QA)}_\text{Maybe} &= –1.95 + (2.58 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Maybe}) + (3.23 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes6mo}) + (3.33 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes30days}) \\ &= –1.95 + (2.58 \cdot 1 ) + (3.23 \cdot 0 ) + (3.33 \cdot 0 ) \\ &= –1.95 + 2.58 \\ &= 0.63 \\ \end{align} \]


For Intent = Yes6mo:

contrasts(quit_data$Intent)['Yes6mo',]
    Maybe    Yes6mo Yes30days 
        0         1         0

So:

\[ \begin{align} \text{logodds(QA)}_\text{Yes6mo} &= –1.95 + (2.58 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Maybe}) + (3.23 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes6mo}) + (3.33 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes30days}) \\ &= –1.95 + (2.58 \cdot 0 ) + (3.23 \cdot 1 ) + (3.33 \cdot 0 ) \\ &= –1.95 + 3.23 \\ &= 1.28 \\ \end{align} \]


For Intent = Yes30days:

contrasts(quit_data$Intent)['Yes30days',]
    Maybe    Yes6mo Yes30days 
        0         0         1

So:

\[ \begin{align} \text{logodds(QA)}_\text{Yes30days} &= –1.95 + (2.58 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Maybe}) + (3.23 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes6mo}) + (3.33 \cdot \text{Intent}_\text{Yes30days}) \\ &= –1.95 + (2.58 \cdot 0 ) + (3.23 \cdot 0 ) + (3.33 \cdot 1 ) \\ &= –1.95 + 3.33 \\ &= 1.38 \\ \end{align} \]

Question 9

Convert each of the log-odds values you calculated in Q8 to probabilities.

How much more probable is it to have a previous quit attempt for someone who intends to quit within the next 30 days, compared to someone who intends never to quit?

🗂️ See Probabilities and log-odds flash card.

Solution

Solution 9. For Intent = Never:

plogis(-1.95) |> round(3) * 100
[1] 12.5

The probability of a previous quit attempt for people who intend never to quit is 12.5%. (notice that this works out to the same value as the model’s intercept, which matches our interpretation above!)


For Intent = Maybe:

plogis(0.63) |> round(3) * 100
[1] 65.2

The probability of a previous quit attempt for people who intend maybe to quit is 65.2%.


For Intent = Yes6mo:

plogis(1.28) |> round(3) * 100
[1] 78.2

The probability of a previous quit attempt for people who intend to quit within the next six months is 78.2%.


For Intent = Yes30days:

plogis(1.38) |> round(3) * 100
[1] 79.9

The probability of a previous quit attempt for people who intend to quit within the next 30 days is 79.9%.

If someone intends to quit within 30 days, they’re 79.9% – 12.5% = 67.4% more likely to have a previous quitting attempt, compared to someone who never intends to quit. (Fun fact: This is how you can calculate the difference between groups in probability space! Not by converting the slope from log-odds to probabilities, but rather by calculating the model-predicted probabilities for the two groups you want to compare, and then subtracting them :) )
