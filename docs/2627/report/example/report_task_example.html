Study background and procedure

In this study, we’re interested in how people perceive inaccurate explanations of psychological phenomena. Specifically, we want to know whether people judge inaccurate explanations to be more accurate if those explanations are accompanied by other kinds of information, and how that effect might be impacted by people’s level of education.

In total, 270 participants were recruited: 90 with no degree, 90 with a UG degree, and 90 with a PG degree. These are the three education groups. The UG and PG degrees were all in cognitive neuroscience, cognitive psychology, or closely related fields.

Participants from each education group were equally assigned into one of three neuroscience information conditions: 90 participants to the “no neuroscience” condition, 90 to “text neuroscience”, and 90 to “visual neuroscience”. In the “no neuroscience” condition, no neuroscience information (i.e., no brain-related information in any format) was provided. In the “text neuroscience” condition, a description of the brain areas involved in the phenomenon was added to each explanation. In the “visual neuroscience” condition, an image showing the brain area(s) involved in the phenomenon was added to each explanation.

Participants took part in a testing block that matched their neuroscience information condition. In this testing block, participants judged the accuracy of 10 inaccurate explanations of psychological phenomena on a scale of 0 (inaccurate) to 10 (accurate). Each participants’ accuracy judgements across the 10 explanations were then summed up, which gives us an accuracy score from 0 to 100 for each person.

Research question and prediction

RQ: Do differences in accuracy judgements between neuroscience conditions depend on people’s level of education?

Specific prediction:

  • People with UG and PG degrees will judge explanations with neuroscientific visuals to be less accurate than people without a degree.

Data dictionary

The data contains:

variable description
PID Participant identifier
educ Education level (ND = no degree; UG = undergraduate degree; PG = postgraduate degree)
neuro Neuroscience condition (None = no neuroscience; Desc = text description; Pic = visual presentation)
O Score on 10 personality items assessing Openness from the IPIP Big Five Measure (z-scored)
C Score on 10 personality items assessing Conscientiousness from the IPIP Big Five Measure (z-scored)
E Score on 10 personality items assessing Extraversion from the IPIP Big Five Measure (z-scored)
A Score on 10 personality items assessing Agreeableness from the IPIP Big Five Measure (z-scored)
N Score on 10 personality items assessing Neuroticism from the IPIP Big Five Measure (z-scored)
acc_rate Accuracy judgement summed across 10 explanations (0 to 100)
